Webhook Notifier

Customer Pain Points & Design Rationale

Executive Summary

The purpose of the Webhook Notifier is not simply to deliver HTTP requests. Its primary business objective is to provide reliable and fair webhook delivery for all customers, even when a small number of customers ("whales") generate extremely high event volumes.

The design focuses on:

  • Reliable delivery (At-Least-Once)
  • Fair scheduling across customers
  • Horizontal scalability
  • Fault tolerance
  • Operational simplicity

Primary Customer Pain Points

# Pain Point Business Impact Example
1 Whale customers monopolize processing Small customers experience very high delivery latency. Customer A generates 200K events/sec while Customer B generates only 5 events/sec.
2 Temporary endpoint failures Webhook events may be permanently lost without retries. Receiver returns HTTP 500 or network timeout.
3 Traffic spikes Large marketing campaigns generate sudden bursts of events. Hundreds of thousands of subscriber updates.
4 Unreliable Internet Transient network failures should not cause data loss. DNS failure, connection refused, TLS timeout.
5 Growing customer base System must continue scaling without redesign. 100 customers today, 20,000 customers tomorrow.
6 Duplicate deliveries Retries naturally produce duplicate webhook deliveries. Timeout followed by successful retry.
7 Slow webhook receivers One customer's failures should not block other customers. Customer B continuously returns HTTP 500.
8 Operational visibility Support engineers need to troubleshoot delivery failures. Retry count, last error, claim owner, timestamps.

Why At-Least-Once Delivery?

Delivery Guarantee Pros Cons
At Most Once No duplicates Events may be permanently lost
At Least Once (Chosen) Reliable delivery Duplicates possible (receiver deduplicates)
Exactly Once Ideal semantics High complexity and unnecessary for assignment scope

Scalability Strategy

Notifier instances are stateless and independently claim work using FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED. This enables horizontal scaling while avoiding duplicate processing.

Known Limitation

Fairness is guaranteed within each notifier instance. Global fairness across multiple notifier instances could be further improved by introducing customer-aware claiming or a centralized scheduler. This additional complexity was considered outside the scope of the assignment.

Design Goal

Deliver webhook events reliably and fairly so that no customer is starved, temporary failures do not cause event loss, and the system can scale horizontally as customer traffic grows.
